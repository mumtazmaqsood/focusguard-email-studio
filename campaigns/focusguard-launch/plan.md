# Campaign plan: FocusGuard launch

**Campaign name:** Time Back, the FocusGuard waitlist launch
**Owner:** Mumtaz
**Dates:** Monday 7 December 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027 (4 weeks)
**Channel:** email only, no paid ads

---

## 1. Campaign overview

**In one sentence:** we invite the 2,000 people on our waitlist to try the FocusGuard test version, and help them get through install and partner setup so they get their time back.

**Main goal (one number, one deadline):**
**300 waitlist members install FocusGuard and finish partner setup by Sunday 3 January 2027.**

That's 15% of the waitlist. We count partner setup, not just installs, because FocusGuard only really works once a partner can give out codes.

**Secondary goals:**
- Collect useful feedback on the test version (bug reports and ideas on GitHub).
- Learn which group (parents, professionals or students) responds best, to guide the next campaign.

---

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the FocusGuard waitlist. They have already raised their hand, so this is a **decision-stage** audience. They need a clear reason to start this month and an easy path through setup.

| Group | Who they are | What they want | What worries them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **parents** | Parents setting FocusGuard up on their child's Android phone. The parent is the partner. | Calmer evenings, homework before social media, fewer arguments about screens | Setup looks technical; their child might switch it off; their child may feel it is unfair |
| **professionals** | Adults who want more focus at work. A spouse or friend is their partner. | Deep work without checking Facebook or X, a clear mind, time back in the day | "I've tried apps before and just turned them off"; installing from a file feels risky |
| **students** | Adult learners (18+) who want more time for reading and study. A friend, sibling or study buddy is their partner. | Study hours without scrolling, more time for reading, better habits before exams | Asking someone to be their partner feels awkward; they want it to be quick |

**Where they spend time:** their inbox (they signed up by email), WhatsApp (partner codes are shared there), and Facebook and X (the very apps they want to limit).

**Note:** the students group is adults only. We don't email children. For under-18s, the parent is our reader.

---

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:**
FocusGuard helps you spend less time on Facebook and X, so you have more time for what matters, with a partner by your side.

**Supporting messages and their proof points.** Every proof point is a true fact from the FocusGuard README or release notes; nothing is made up.

| # | Message | Proof point |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One calm daily limit.** Facebook, X and anything you add share one daily limit. | 60 minutes a day by default, in Chrome and in the apps. You can add other websites and apps to the block list. |
| 2 | **Allowed hours, so the evening stays yours.** | Blocked sites and apps only work in your allowed hours (14:00–19:00 by default). You pick the hours in 30-minute steps. |
| 3 | **A partner keeps you honest, kindly.** | Only your partner can give you more time, with a one-time code from Google Authenticator, which you ask for on WhatsApp. Making the rules looser needs a partner code; making them stricter doesn't. |
| 4 | **Private by design.** | FocusGuard has no internet permission, no ads and no analytics. Nothing leaves your phone. |

**Message angle by group (same facts, different opening):**
- **parents:** "Homework first, scrolling later, without the nightly argument."
- **professionals:** "Give your best hours to your work, not your feed."
- **students:** "More time for reading and study, one day at a time."

**Always say honestly:** it's a **test version**, it runs on **Android 8.0 or newer**, websites are checked in **Chrome** only, and people install it from a file (an APK) on GitHub, not the Play Store.

## 7. Success metrics

**Main KPI:** 300 people who installed FocusGuard **and** finished partner setup by Sunday 3 January 2027.

**How we'll measure it honestly:** FocusGuard has no internet permission and no analytics, so the app can't report installs. We'll count it in two ways:
1. **Replies:** emails 02–04 ask readers to reply "done" once their partner is set up. Count unique "done" replies.
2. **Download count:** the GitHub release asset download count, read from the GitHub releases page, as an upper estimate of installs.

We report replies as the main number and downloads as context. We never claim more than the counts show.

**Secondary KPIs:**

| KPI | Target | How we measure it |
|---|---|---|
| APK downloads during the campaign | Set after week 1, from real data | GitHub release asset download count |
| Clicks on the main link, per email | Set after week 1, from real data | Email service click reports |
| Clicks per group (parents, professionals, students) | Compare the groups; no fixed target | Email service click reports, split by group |
| Feedback received | At least one bug report or idea per week | GitHub issues and email replies |
| Unsubscribes | Kept low; watch for any spike after a send | Email service reports |

We don't set targets for opens and clicks in advance, because we have no past campaign data. We set them from week 1's real numbers.

**Reporting:** a short note every Wednesday (the day after each send), and a wrap-up on Monday 4 January 2027.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | What we'll do |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup is hard.** Installing an APK, the Play Protect warning and the Android 13+ "Restricted setting" step can stop people. | Our goal counts finished setups, not downloads. | Email 02 is all about setup, with the exact fix written out. Link to the install guide. Invite replies for help. |
| **We can't see installs.** The app has no analytics, on purpose. | We might not know if we hit the goal. | Ask for "done" replies and use GitHub download counts. Report honestly what we can and can't see. |
| **Some readers use iPhone.** FocusGuard is Android only. | They can't take part, and may feel left out. | Say "Android 8.0 or newer" clearly in email 01. Don't promise an iPhone or Play Store version. |
| **It's a test version and may have bugs.** | Bad first impressions. | Be open that it's a test version. Point people to the GitHub Bug report form. |
| **The holiday inbox is busy.** | Emails 03 and 04 may get less attention. | Send on Tuesdays, keep the emails short, and use a calm tone with no countdowns or pressure. |
